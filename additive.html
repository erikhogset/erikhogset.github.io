Additive Manufacturing Projects

FFF and SLA projects exploring print strategy, tolerancing, structural performance, and design for additive manufacturing.

FFF SLA Print Tuning ME557

Overview

These projects were completed as part of ME557 Additive Manufacturing at Boston University. Together they explore different additive manufacturing methods and design strategies, including articulated FFF prints, SLA geometry, generative design, and functional printed mechanisms.

Across these builds I focused on print orientation, tolerancing, support strategy, post-processing, and designing parts that take advantage of the capabilities of additive manufacturing rather than conventional fabrication.

Flying Sea Turtle — FFF Print & Assembly

Multi-part print produced on a Creality Ender-3 FFF printer, focusing on printer calibration, assembly tolerances, and post-print assembly rather than original part design.

Design credit: amaochan.work — original STL design. My contribution focused on fabrication, print tuning, and successful operation.

Motion demo showing assembly motion

Technical Notes

What I tuned on the printer and what changed print-to-print.

Technical Notes

This print focused on calibration and process control rather than geometry design. Print orientation was selected to place layer lines along the primary joint axes, improving articulation durability and reducing the likelihood of layer separation during motion.

Support structures were minimized around the joint regions to prevent binding after removal. Printer tuning included adjusting extrusion flow, temperature, and retraction to achieve consistent joint clearances and smooth articulation without modifying the original STL.

Klein Bottle — SLA Design & Print

SLA-printed Klein bottle designed to fit within a 40 × 40 × 40 mm size constraint and demonstrate a continuous internal channel impossible to machine using conventional subtractive manufacturing. Printed in Formlabs Draft Grey Resin.

Technical Notes

Design intent, print strategy, and post-processing decisions.

What I Focused On

  • CAD modeling of the internal feature within the printed geometry
  • Print orientation and support reduction to limit support marks
  • Sanding and post-processing to improve finish, with minor surface scarring

Technical Notes

The Klein bottle geometry was modeled to maintain a continuous internal channel while remaining within the 40 × 40 × 40 mm build volume constraint. Print orientation was selected to minimize supports within the interior passage and reduce surface artifacts on the exterior curvature.

After printing, the part underwent washing, UV curing, and light sanding to remove support marks and improve surface finish. Backlighting was used to visually confirm the continuity of the internal channel, demonstrating a geometry that cannot be produced using conventional subtractive methods.

Mass–Performance Structure — Fusion Generative Design, FFF Print & Compression Test

Compression structure developed by defining a full allowable design space between constrained faces, then using Autodesk Fusion 360 Generative Design to remove material while preserving load paths. The resulting geometry was manufactured via FFF in PETG and validated under compressive loading, successfully withstanding a 2000 N compressive load.

Final PETG print produced from the Fusion generative design result.

Technical Notes

Design space definition, loading setup, and print strategy.

What I Focused On

  • Defining an appropriate design space spanning the constrained faces
  • Applying compression loads and boundary conditions in Fusion Generative Design
  • Orienting print layers to support compressive load paths

Technical Notes

A design space was defined between two constrained faces representing the load application surfaces. Compressive loading conditions and fixed boundary constraints were applied in Fusion 360 Generative Design to identify material layouts capable of sustaining the required load.

The resulting geometry was printed in PETG with layer orientation aligned with the primary compressive load direction to reduce the risk of layer separation. The final structure successfully sustained a 2 kN compressive load during testing without structural failure.

Anemometer + Weather Vane — FFF Design & Print

Functional anemometer and vane assembly printed in PLA. The design emphasizes bearing integration, press-fit tolerances, low-friction rotation, and stable vane alignment under low-speed airflow.

Wind tunnel test demonstrating vane alignment and rotor response under airflow.

Technical Notes

Bearing fit, shaft alignment, and airflow-driven rotation.

What I Focused On

  • Press-fit tolerances for bearing integration
  • Low-friction rotation and shaft alignment
  • Balancing rotating mass for smooth response

Technical Notes

The rotor assembly was designed around press-fit bearings to allow low-friction rotation without additional fastening hardware. Shaft alignment and bearing tolerances were tuned to ensure smooth rotation while maintaining sufficient interference to prevent bearing movement during operation.

Mass distribution across the rotor arms was balanced to reduce vibration and improve responsiveness under low airflow conditions. During wind tunnel testing the vane reliably aligned with the flow direction and the rotor rotated smoothly with minimal friction.
