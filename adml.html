Compact Fishing Kit

A compact, pocket-sized fishing kit developed for ME345 Automation & Manufacturing Methods. This project combined product design, CAM planning, ADML routing, and automated manufacturing system analysis to produce a functional two-part kit consisting of a machined body and lid.

ME345 ADML CNC Machining Automation Manufacturing Strategy

Project Overview

Our team designed a compact fishing kit intended to improve portability, organization, and safety compared to bulkier or poorly compartmentalized alternatives. The final product consists of a main body and lid, with dedicated regions for fishing essentials and design details that improve usability.

Product Design

The kit was designed as a two-part assembly consisting of a machined main body and lid. Functional features included separate regions for hooks and sinkers, a perimeter groove for fishing line, and assist tabs to make opening and closing easier.

  • Two-part design: body + lid
  • Dedicated hook and sinker storage
  • Perimeter line groove
  • Assist tabs for opening and closing
  • Designed around ADML machining and assembly constraints

Manufacturing Strategy

The body and lid were machined as separate parts and planned to fit within the ADML workflow. The body emphasized pocketing, contouring, T-slot machining, and chamfering, while the lid required hole machining, chamfering, and engraving operations.

ADML Routing & System Plan

A major part of this project was planning how the product would move through the ADML system. The routing strategy coordinated pallets, AGVs, machining at Stations 3 and 4, inventory transfer, and final assembly at Station 1.

  • Parallel routing for lid and body operations
  • Material transport using AGVs and pallet buffers
  • Intermediate inventory handling before assembly
  • Final assembly performed after both machined components were complete

Key Takeaways

Design

This project strengthened my ability to design around both product function and manufacturing constraints, especially when features had to work within tooling, tolerance, and assembly limits.

Manufacturing Systems

I gained experience translating a part design into a complete production workflow involving CAM, routing logic, machine allocation, and assembly sequencing inside an automated lab environment.
