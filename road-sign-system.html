Compact Fishing Kit Prototype

Compact two-part product developed for ME345 Automation & Manufacturing Methods, combining CNC process planning, product design, robotic handling, and automated manufacturing cell integration.

ME345 Automation & Manufacturing CNC Machining ADML Product Design

System Overview

This project was completed as part of a 6-person team in Boston University’s Automated Design and Manufacturing Laboratory (ADML). The objective was to design a functional product and develop an automated manufacturing workflow capable of producing it through CNC machining, robotic handling, pallet routing, and final assembly.

The final workflow combined CAD design, CAM toolpaths, robot waypoints, intermediate inventory, and BUMES cell-level control logic to coordinate the full ADML production sequence.

Final Product

The compact fishing kit is a portable two-part product consisting of a machined body and lid. It stores hooks, sinkers, and fishing line in a compact form while remaining easy to open, close, and carry.

My Role

My work focused on turning the product concept into a manufacturable and automatable system. I led much of the machining strategy, automation logic integration, robot coordination support, and later-stage debugging with my teammate Ranely.

  • Developed CNC toolpaths and process plans for the body and lid components.
  • Helped define robot waypoints and supported robot programming within the ADML workflow.
  • Created final cell-level control logic coordinating machining, robot motion, routing, and assembly using BUMES.
  • Supported tolerance adjustments, hole sizing decisions, and manufacturability improvements.
  • Led later-stage integration, debugging, and testing to improve system reliability.

Product Design

The design consists of a main body with storage features for hooks, sinkers, and fishing line, plus a machined lid with an engraved fish graphic and alignment holes for assembly. Because the part had to move through an automated cell, design decisions also had to account for machining accessibility, tolerances, and downstream handling.

Manufacturing Strategy

The body and lid were planned as separate machining workflows. The body required pocketing, contouring, and chamfering operations, while the lid required hole machining, chamfering, and front-side engraving.

ADML Routing Plan

The routing plan coordinated machining, robotic handling, pallet transport, intermediate inventory, and final assembly. The product had to function not only as a physical object, but also as a part moving through a multi-step automated manufacturing environment.

ADML Manufacturing & Assembly

The final workflow integrated CNC machining, collaborative robot handling, pallet transport, and automated assembly into a coordinated production sequence. This required aligning machining paths, robot motions, routing logic, and BUMES control logic so the product could move through the cell reliably.

Engineering Challenges

Manufacturing & Tolerancing

Feature sizing, hole geometry, and tolerances had to work for both machining and later handling/assembly. Design decisions had to support real production constraints rather than only part appearance in CAD.

Automation & Integration

Machining plans, robot waypoints, routing logic, and cell-level control software had to work together as one coordinated manufacturing cell rather than as isolated operations.

Outcome & Takeaways

The project resulted in a completed compact fishing kit prototype and a full automated manufacturing strategy for producing it within the ADML environment. It strengthened my experience in CNC process planning, manufacturable product design, tolerancing, robot integration, automation logic development, and system-level manufacturing planning.

Design for Manufacturing

The project reinforced how part geometry must align with tooling, fixturing, tolerances, and assembly requirements.

Automation Planning

The work connected product design to CAM, routing logic, robot coordination, and automated assembly sequencing.
