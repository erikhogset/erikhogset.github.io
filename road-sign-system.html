Reactive Road Sign System

Senior Design Project — Mechanical Retrofit for Hurricane-Resistant Road Signs

Project Overview

The Reactive Road Sign project is a mechanical retrofit device designed to reduce structural damage to roadside signs during extreme wind events such as hurricanes. Conventional road signs are rigidly mounted, which allows aerodynamic loads to transfer directly into the post and foundation. Under severe wind loading, this frequently leads to permanent deformation or complete structural failure of the signpost.

This project introduces a passive mechanical system that redirects wind loads through a controlled response mechanism. Under moderate winds, the sign is able to rotate slightly to align with wind direction, reducing aerodynamic loading. Under extreme wind conditions, a sacrificial breakaway pin fractures in a controlled manner, allowing the sign to fold downward and preventing damage to the post or foundation. After the storm event, the system can be restored by simply replacing the breakaway pin rather than replacing the entire sign structure.

The system is designed as an inline retrofit kit that integrates directly into existing roadside infrastructure. Installation requires cutting the signpost and inserting the device between the upper and lower sections of the post, preserving standardized sign geometries and mounting heights required by transportation regulations.

Status: In Progress — Current work focuses on structural validation, prototype fabrication, and breakaway pin testing.

Mechanical Design

The mechanism contains two primary subsystems that govern the sign’s response to increasing wind loads.

Torsional Spring Subsystem

A torsional spring located within the base coupler allows limited rotational motion about the signpost axis. When wind forces exceed a defined trigger load, the sign rotates up to 15° in either direction. This motion reduces the effective projected area of the sign and decreases aerodynamic loading on the structure.

Breakaway U-Joint Subsystem

Above the spring subsystem, a universal joint connects the upper and lower portions of the signpost. Under normal conditions, the joint is constrained by a sacrificial breakaway pin. When wind forces exceed the ultimate design load, the pin fractures, releasing the constraint and allowing the sign to fold downward into a protected configuration.

Engineering Analysis

Analytical modeling was used to relate wind loading on the sign face to internal stresses within the breakaway pin. Wind pressure was converted into a resultant force acting at the centroid of the sign panel, producing a moment about the joint.

This moment was translated into an equivalent transverse force acting on the breakaway pin. The pin was modeled as a short beam subjected to combined bending and shear loading, allowing stresses to be estimated using classical beam theory.

Analytical model relating wind force on the sign face to bending and shear stresses within the sacrificial pin.

Finite Element Analysis

Finite element analysis was conducted using COMSOL Multiphysics to evaluate stress distributions within the breakaway pin under ultimate wind loading conditions. The objective of the simulation was not to fully model fracture mechanics but rather to identify peak stress locations and validate the analytical predictions of bending-dominated loading behavior.

The simulations applied equivalent wind loads derived from aerodynamic calculations to the joint assembly. Stress distributions within the pin were evaluated across multiple pin diameters to determine a geometry that would fail predictably at the target load threshold while keeping surrounding components below their failure limits.

FEA results showing stress concentrations within the sacrificial breakaway pin used to guide pin sizing and material selection.

Prototype Development

A physical proof-of-concept prototype was fabricated to validate the mechanical behavior of the joint mechanism. The prototype demonstrated that the universal joint system can successfully constrain motion under normal conditions while allowing controlled release once the breakaway pin is removed.

Early prototype used to validate joint motion and mechanical feasibility of the breakaway mechanism.
