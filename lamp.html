Smart Desk Lamp

A smart lighting prototype designed to improve accessibility and user comfort through sensor-based interaction, adaptive brightness control, and time-based blue-light reduction.

Product Design Sensors Microcontrollers Prototyping

Prototype Demonstration

This project explored how a desk lamp could become more accessible and responsive by combining mechanical product design with sensing and control logic.

  • Gesture input: an ultrasonic sensor detects a hand wave to cycle between operating modes.
  • Five operating modes: dim, normal, bright, automatic brightness, and off.
  • Adaptive lighting: an ambient light sensor adjusts output automatically in changing room conditions.
  • Blue-light reduction: a real-time clock changes lighting behavior over the course of the day.
  • Mechanical adjustability: the lamp head can be repositioned manually with low effort.

The final prototype demonstrates a complete physical system rather than only an electronics proof of concept.

Problem

Standard desk lamps often rely on small manual switches and fixed brightness settings, which can make them less convenient for users with limited dexterity and less responsive to changing lighting conditions. They also do little to account for visual comfort throughout the day.

This project was developed to explore a more user-centered lighting system that could respond to gesture input, room brightness, and time of day while still functioning as a stable and adjustable physical product.

Design Goals

User Experience Goals

  • Reduce reliance on small manual controls
  • Allow simple mode changes through gesture input
  • Improve accessibility for users with limited dexterity
  • Provide lighting modes suitable for different desk tasks

Engineering Goals

  • Integrate sensing, control, and mechanical adjustment into one system
  • Create a stable lamp base and adjustable upper assembly
  • Use sensor feedback to automatically adapt brightness
  • Incorporate time-based control logic to reduce blue-light output later in the day

My Role

My primary contribution was the mechanical and product design of the lamp system, while also supporting broader integration of the sensing and electronics into the final prototype. I was responsible for developing the CAD architecture, refining the adjustable head mechanism, and helping ensure the physical design could accommodate the overall control system.

  • Designed the full mechanical assembly in CAD, including the base, vertical support structure, and adjustable lamp head.
  • Iterated through multiple hinge concepts to balance smooth motion with stable positioning of the lamp head.
  • Selected and implemented a lock-washer friction hinge that keeps the head in place while still requiring low force to reposition.
  • Contributed to overall system integration by helping package the sensing hardware, electronics, and mechanical components into a coherent product layout.
  • Supported prototype refinement by evaluating usability, adjustability, and how the physical design worked with the sensing features.

System Design

Interaction & Control

  • Ultrasonic sensor detects a hand wave to change operating mode.
  • Ambient light sensor supports automatic brightness adjustment.
  • Real-time clock enables time-based blue-light reduction behavior.
  • Microcontroller coordinates sensor input and lamp output logic.
  • The final prototype required close integration between the control features and the physical lamp architecture.

Mechanical Product Design

  • Stable base supports the electronics and lower housing.
  • Vertical support structure connects the base to the adjustable head.
  • An iterated hinge design allows manual repositioning while maintaining head stability.
  • The final form factor was designed to house hardware while maintaining a clean and functional desk-lamp profile.

Final Prototype

The final prototype integrated the physical lamp structure, adjustable head, sensing hardware, and control electronics into a single functional assembly.

Engineering Challenges

Physical Integration

  • Packaging sensors and control hardware into a compact desk-lamp form.
  • Balancing a stable base with an adjustable upper assembly.
  • Maintaining a design that felt like a usable product rather than a bench-top prototype.

Mechanism & Usability

  • Developing a head adjustment mechanism that felt smooth but still held position reliably.
  • Balancing low repositioning force with sufficient holding friction.
  • Ensuring the sensing features improved usability without overwhelming the physical product design.

Prototype Development

Earlier iterations were used to validate the concept, test the interaction style, and evaluate both lighting behavior and physical adjustability before the final integrated design was completed.

CAD & Mechanical Design

CAD development focused on turning the concept into a physically coherent product, with attention given to overall assembly layout, internal hardware packaging, and the adjustable head mechanism. A major part of the design process was iterating on the hinge connection to achieve both stable positioning and low-effort user adjustment.

Outcome

This project resulted in a working smart lamp prototype that combined mechanical design, embedded sensing, and user-focused functionality in a single system. It also served as one of my earliest full product design projects, helping build my interest in physical prototyping, sensor integration, and user-centered engineering.

The project strengthened my experience in CAD development, mechanism iteration, product integration, and designing hardware that had to function both as a physical object and as an interactive device.
