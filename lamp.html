Smart Desk Lamp

A sensor-driven desk lamp prototype designed to improve accessibility, adjust brightness automatically, and reduce blue-light exposure over the course of the day.

Product Design Sensors Microcontrollers Prototyping

Prototype Demonstration

This prototype was developed as a smart desk lamp that adapts to both the user and the surrounding environment.

  • Ultrasonic sensor: detects a hand wave and cycles through the lamp’s operating modes.
  • Five modes: dim, normal, bright, automatic brightness, and off.
  • Ambient light sensor: adjusts output automatically in auto mode based on room brightness.
  • Real-time clock: reduces blue-light output over the course of the day for warmer evening lighting.
  • Adjustable head: allows the lamp to be repositioned manually with low effort.

The final system combines physical product design with sensing and control logic to create a more accessible and adaptive lighting experience. [oai_citation:2‡EK210_Final Report.pdf](sediment://file_00000000fe3c722fb7c8b08641a7c334)

Project Overview

The goal of this project was to design a desk lamp that was easier to use and better suited to a user’s environment than a standard manually operated lamp. Instead of relying only on switches or fixed brightness settings, the lamp uses sensing and control logic to respond dynamically to user input, room brightness, and time of day. [oai_citation:3‡EK210_Final Report.pdf](sediment://file_00000000fe3c722fb7c8b08641a7c334)

The design focused on three main objectives: improving accessibility for users with limited dexterity, providing automatic brightness adjustment, and reducing blue-light exposure later in the day. These goals shaped both the physical design of the lamp and the logic used to control its output. What the Lamp Does

User Interaction

  • Waving a hand over the sensor changes lamp mode
  • Modes cycle through dim, normal, bright, automatic, and off
  • Designed to reduce reliance on small manual controls
  • Lamp head can be repositioned with minimal force

Adaptive Lighting

  • Ambient light sensor adjusts output automatically in auto mode
  • Brightness levels correspond to dim, normal, and bright desk lighting targets
  • Real-time clock gradually lowers blue-light percentage later in the day
  • System balances usability, visibility, and comfort

Sensing & Control Logic

Sensors & Inputs

  • Ultrasonic motion sensor for gesture-based user input
  • Ambient light sensor for room brightness detection
  • Real-time clock for time-dependent color adjustment

System Response

  • Wave input advances the lamp through operating states
  • Auto mode maps room lux level to dim, normal, or bright output
  • LED output shifts to lower blue-light content in the evening
  • Microcontroller coordinates sensing, logic, and final light output

In the final design, the lamp used time-of-day logic to shift from a high blue-light percentage in the morning to a much warmer output at night, while maintaining useful brightness levels across its modes. [oai_citation:6‡EK210_Final Report.pdf](sediment://file_00000000fe3c722fb7c8b08641a7c334)

Mechanical & Product Design

The lamp was designed as a functional desktop product rather than only an electronics demonstration. The form integrated a stable base, vertical support structure, adjustable head, and internal routing for wires and components. The head mechanism was developed to hold position while still requiring only a small amount of force to move.
